# Research log

## Origin

A conversation (chats/chat.md) and a research dossier written in it (chats/1976-2036_research_dossier.md, with a .docx copy of the same text). The conversation asked when "computer" last described a person, proposed 1976 as a symbolic date, proposed 2036 for "mathematician", moved between 2032 and 2036, and then asked for a deep study. The dossier corrected the earlier preference for 2032, proposed five transitions, four scenarios and a preregistration design, audited the 2026 evidence and listed 34 sources. It named its own priority: a pilot of the semantic-default measurement together with an audited registry of research episodes.

## Questions

1. When did the unqualified "computer" come to mean a machine in print, and how does that date relate to 1946, the end of human computing work and 1976?
2. Is there a second case of a person-to-machine noun, and how did it resolve?
3. What does "mathematician" look like in 2026 by the same measure?
4. What do the 2026 reports on machine mathematics establish, and with which caveats?
5. Which distinctions (task, autonomy, production, jurisdiction, word; understanding, control) are needed to state a falsifiable claim for 2036?

Retrieved 8 October 2026, frozen in simulation/data/ngram.json.
- OpenAlex works API, title-and-abstract search grouped by publication year, 2016 to 2026, for "mathematician" and qualified forms; and every work matching "human mathematician" with flags computed from title and abstract. Retrieved 8 October 2026, frozen in simulation/data/. A single author's self-deposited series (27 of the 84 records of 2026, 18 after removing duplicate titles) used the phrase in a boilerplate line and was excluded in the cleaned count; duplicate titles were removed throughout.

## Findings from the literature

- [T1] Light 1999 (abstract): nearly two hundred women worked as human computers on the ENIAC project; six were selected to program the machine that took their name and replaced them, and the machine's expertise became more celebrated than theirs. McLennan and Gainer (NASA page, read): before electronic computers "computer" was a job title for people; hundreds of women, most with degrees in mathematics or sciences, worked at Langley; African-American women were grouped in the segregated West Area Computers.
- [T1] Appel and Haken 1976 (abstract): every planar map is four colorable; full proof in two papers, the second with Koch. Newell and Simon 1956 (abstract): a system that discovers proofs in symbolic logic by heuristic methods. McCune (research record, read): EQP found the Robbins proof on 10 October 1996. Dick 2011 (abstract): the AURA system at Argonne; hybrid human-machine contributions and a transformation of mathematical intuition. Hales et al. 2017 (abstract): formal proof of the Kepler conjecture in HOL Light and Isabelle. Barendregt and Wiedijk 2005 (abstract): humans define and prove, machines check.
- [T1] Rav 1999 (abstract): the epistemic content of proofs exceeds theorem statements. Thurston 1994 (abstract): an essay on proof and progress responding to Jaffe and Quinn. DeMillo, Lipton and Perlis 1979 (abstract): formal verification of programs will not play the role that proofs play in mathematics. Paulson 2025 (essay, read): their main argument was wrong; formal proof has entered mathematics. Avigad 2008 (opening): what it means to understand mathematics.
- [T1] Autor 2015 (abstract): automation substitutes for and complements labor. Acemoglu and Restrepo 2019 (abstract): displacement and the creation of new tasks. Ochigame 2024 (abstract): automation, proof standards, labor and credit.
- [T1] Haspelmath 2006 (abstract): markedness reduces to frequency asymmetries. Gygax et al. 2008 (abstract): stereotypes and grammar shape the gender readers infer for role nouns. Hamilton et al. 2016 (abstract): quantitative laws of semantic change. Pechenick et al. 2015 (abstract): the Google Books corpus is a library with one copy of each book, and a single prolific author can insert phrases. Younes and Reips 2019 (abstract): reliability procedures for Ngram studies.
- [T1] Feng et al. 2026 (arXiv abstract): Aletheia; a paper generated without human intervention, a human-AI collaboration, a semi-autonomous evaluation of 700 open Erdős problems with autonomous solutions to four; proposes autonomy and novelty levels and human-AI interaction cards. Tsoukalas et al. 2026 (arXiv abstract): 9 of 353 open Erdős problems at a few hundred dollars per problem, 44 of 492 OEIS conjectures, Lean-verified. OpenAI repository (README and history, read): 719 manuscripts in 372 families from about 4,000 posed problems, about three hours of compute per result, about 42% of top-line results formalized; on 7 October three withdrawals after a sign error and 14 revised manuscripts. Leiden Declaration (text, read): 2 June 2026, IMU endorsement, credit and responsibility belong to humans. AGMAI (text, read): 29 September 2026, over 600 replies, a two-tier system, papers not yet understood by anybody. Tao 2026 (arXiv abstract): ICM 2026 lecture on the goals and values of mathematical research. Enayat 2026 (arXiv abstract): Benjamin, Rav and Wittgenstein.

## Decisions

- Kept from the dossier: the five transitions and two outcomes, the background/episode distinction, the units of comparison, verification versus fidelity, novelty, significance and understanding, jurisdiction, the hours identity, open results versus open means, the apprenticeship question, extension versus default versus exclusion, the four scenarios, the three preregistered claims.
- Added: a corpus dating of the first transfer and its comparison case; the 2026 corpus baseline for "mathematician"; the distinction between contrastive and default marking, measured; Lakatos 1976 and the AM thesis as further 1976 events.
- Changed: the human-computer timeline is stated from NASA's own page (1935 to 1970) and Light; the chat's single-source claims about a NASA historian's interview and individual careers are not used. The Harvard terminology page and the OpenAI announcement page could not be read (JavaScript) and are not cited; the repository is cited instead.
- Dropped: the 2030, 2032 and 2040 to 2050 estimates of the conversation, as unsupported dates.

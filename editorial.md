# Editorial record

Status: full read and revision complete (2026-10-08).

## Contribution and reader

For historians and sociologists of computing and mathematics, philosophers of mathematical practice, linguists of semantic change and the mathematical community deciding how to respond to machine research: a dated measurement of the first transfer (when "computer" changed its default referent in print, by three criteria), a second case of the same ambiguity, a 2026 baseline for "mathematician" by the same measure, a separation of five transitions and two outcomes, a reading of the verified 2026 record and professional norms against them, and a protocol of three falsifiable claims for 2036. Confidence is that of corpus measurements on two named indexes with stated biases and of sources checked against their texts; no forecast of a year is made.

## Material worth preserving

From the origin conversation and dossier: the title and its two dates; the warning against treating human computers as clerks; 1936 as a prologue and 1976 as a symbol of authority over proof; the overlapping ambitions (Logic Theory Machine, AM, EQP); the five transitions, the background/episode distinction and the units of comparison; validity, fidelity, novelty, significance and understanding; value versus scarcity; jurisdiction; the hours identity; open results versus open means; apprenticeship; extension, default and exclusion; four scenarios; three preregistered claims; the dossier's own call for a semantic-default pilot, which this paper carries out on corpora.

## Strongest rival

That the noun is irrelevant and the substance is capability: once machines do most of the mathematics, what they are called is a matter of taste. The paper answers that the noun indexes jurisdiction and recognition, which the history of human computers shows can move independently of the work and with consequences for whose contributions count; and that the corpus measure is a cheap, early and falsifiable indicator. A second rival is the practice-centred view that producing results never defined the profession; the paper keeps that view in sections 6 and 7 and measures what it predicts.
